body.glb - 3D human body model (skin-covered athletic figure with muscle groups)
=====================================

body.glb is an adapted work (a smooth skin surface generated from the muscle and bone meshes,
split into 16 named groups, simplified, re-normalised and quantised) of the following openly
licensed datasets:

1) Z-Anatomy - Muscular system and Skeletal system models
   Files used: MuscularSystem100.fbx, SkeletalSystem100.fbx
   Source:  https://github.com/LluisV/Z-Anatomy  (branch PC-Version, Resources/Models/FBX)
            project: https://github.com/Z-Anatomy
   Authors: Z-Anatomy project (Gauthier Kervyn, Lluis Vinent Juanico and contributors)
   License: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0)
            https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

2) BodyParts3D (Z-Anatomy's models are derived from it)
   Source:  https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/download.html
   License page: https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/lic.html
   License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
            https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
   (BodyParts3D was formerly distributed under CC BY-SA 2.1 JP; the current license
   page, checked 2026-11-11, states CC Attribution 4.0 International.)

Changes made: skeletal muscles and bones (no organs, vessels, nerves or fasciae) were
voxelised and wrapped in a continuous skin surface (morphological closing with a soft-tissue
offset, Gaussian smoothing, iso-surface extraction, Taubin smoothing), so the individual
muscles are no longer separate meshes. The skull and face were replaced by a smooth,
bald, faceless sculpted head (generated, not taken from the datasets) and the head was
scaled down slightly. The surface was simplified (meshoptimizer) to about 108k triangles
and split into the regions chest, shoulders, biceps, triceps, forearms, abs, obliques,
traps, lats, lowerback, glutes, quads, adductors, hams, calves, plus "body", by projecting
each skin area onto the nearest Z-Anatomy muscle (with the oblique aponeuroses over the
rectus abdominis and the lumbar latissimus aponeurosis cut away for that projection).
The model was rescaled to 2 units tall.
